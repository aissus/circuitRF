AN-05 — A fast stand-in for a slow device

A compiled transistor model can be the slowest part of a loadpull. At one bias region, one parameter set and one frequency, circuitRF can sample it into a table model of itself, a stand-in that runs in-process and in parallel and answers for any source and load. How to make one, how wide to sample, what it keeps and loses, and how much faster and how close it is on four devices.

When this note applies

A loadpull asks the same transistor the same questions thousands of times: a grid of loads, each driven up step by step into compression, each step a harmonic-balance solve of several Newton iterations. When the transistor is a compiled compact model, every one of those evaluations runs in a separate worker process, one call at a time, through a module with internal nodes of its own. That is often where the time goes.

A stand-in replaces the device with a table model of itself: currents and charges sampled by circuitRF from the device it already runs, fitted by Model Composer, exported as a .cmodel and placed as a ComposedFET. It runs inside circuitRF, so a loadpull grid can solve its loads in parallel, and it has two or three unknowns where a compiled module has many. It is valid at the bias region, the model parameters and the frequency it was sampled at. Inside that region it answers for any source and load, which is what loadpull and harmonicaRF ask.

It is a device-level model, a .cmodel with currents, charges and optionally a thermal network, not a behavioural model of the amplifier. Everything that runs a ComposedFET runs it unchanged.

The flow

Step Command What happens
Sample model sample pa.cmc --from pa.charm --vgs -20,12,0.5 --vds -4,116,2 DC and S at every bias of the grid, on the device's own terminals. Written as sampled/pa/tables.npy (each document has a folder of its own) and imported as a Simulated tables set. With a .charm, the device is also load-pulled over five loads about the document's load, into a Large signal set. Current and Charge are set to tables.
Fit model fit pa.cmc --step all The tables, splined through the samples themselves.
Export model export pa.cmc -o pa.cmodel The stand-in, with what it was sampled from in its provenance.
Validate model validate pa.cmc --model pa.cmodel The stand-in load-pulled over the device's own loads and compared at compression: Pout, Gt, PAE, drain efficiency, AM–PM and Zin.

The .charm supplies the device (its DUT; the package stays in the document), the bias, the fundamental and the terminations, so the loadpull the stand-in is judged by is the one harmonicaRF would run. Without one, --device takes an instance line on the nets g, d and 0 and --freq the small-signal frequency. Then there is no loadpull to validate against, so run the device's own lp and import its .spl as a Large signal set.

Then place it where the device was: in a schematic as ComposedFET:X1 g d s File="pa.cmodel", or in harmonicaRF with Set DUT ▸ Model Composer.

How wide, and how fine

Sample the swing, not the bias. Inside its grid the stand-in is the samples; outside, it is continued linearly, as any table is. The drain swings to about twice its supply, and, unless a diode clamps it, the gate swings both ways by several volts. The routine gate of this feature is the Hero-2 GaN-like FET: a gate that is a 50 Ω resistor, at −3.05 V and 48 V. Sampled over its DC range, vgs −8 to 2 V, at compression it misses by 0.51 dB in Pout and a Zin VSWR of 1.72. Sampled from −20 to 12 V, the same 1 V step gives 0.10 dB and 1.02.

A run that leaves the sampled region warns once, when the run is over, naming the region and how many of the run's operating points fell outside it, and model validate repeats it beside the loadpull it happened in. The count is of every operating point the run evaluated: every drive step of every load, every Newton iterate. A swing that leaves the table only as it nears compression is therefore a small share of the whole. On Hero-2's DC-range grid it was 8 % (4,609 of 55,476 over five loads), and that was enough to miss by half a decibel. Treat any warning in a loadpull as a reason to widen the grid, then validate again; a run that never warns needs nothing.

Fine where the device bends. A table is a cubic spline through the samples, so it follows a curve as well as the samples resolve it. A FET's knee and pinch-off want a few tenths of a volt; an exponential diode or junction wants a few millivolts where it conducts. The Current step reports the step it sees and the swing below which low-level intermodulation is unreliable. Points cost little: sampling is seconds for an in-process device, and a few seconds to a minute for a compiled one, done once.

What it keeps and what it loses

It keeps the device's DC currents at every sampled point exactly, its small-signal behaviour at the sampled frequency, and its large-signal behaviour wherever the device is quasi-static: currents and charges that are functions of the instantaneous terminal voltages.

It loses:

  • Everything that is not quasi-static. Internal nodes collapse into the two-port: a gate resistance with its charge, an inductance, a distributed base, a transit-time excess phase, a non-quasi-static network. Their phase at the working frequency and its harmonics is gone. On the shipped GaN example, whose network is inside the compiled module, this is the largest error below. To keep a known network, create the document with it first (model new pa.cmc --kind active --network …) and type its values; sample locks them and the samples are then de-embedded through it.
  • Charges that are not charges. The charges come from the low-frequency Y, Im Y / ω. When series resistances are inside the sampled device those are not exactly derivatives of two charge functions, and the Charge step's two integration paths disagree: it reports by how much. A few per cent is worth refitting the charges as an ANN, which finds the nearest charges that conserve charge: model fit pa.cmc --step charge --method ANN.
  • Low-level distortion from a table. A cubic table has a piecewise-constant third derivative, so it cannot predict high-order intermodulation at very low drive. Refit that constituent as an ANN where it matters.
  • Traps, unless they are sampled as the reference describes. A quasi-static sample gives each bias its DC trap state.
  • Scaling. A stand-in is one instance. Placing it with N other than N0 is refused; sample the size you use.

Self-heating

When the device's instance line names a fourth net t (its thermal node), sample holds it at 0 K rise while it samples, so the tables are isothermal. It then lets it go to the device's own thermal network at the bias and reads Rth as the rise over the power at the terminals. With two or more --temps that Rth is typed on the Thermal step, self-heating on. Type Cth (or the time constant) there yourself before fitting.

The stand-in's Rth is not quite the module's. A compact model heats with its core's power; the stand-in's core is the whole device, so it heats with the terminals' power, which also includes what the series resistances dissipate. On the GaN module the module's own 25 K/W reads 22.8 K/W at its hottest sample, and with that value the stand-in's DC with self-heating is within 0.22 % of the original's.

A device whose thermal node is not reachable is sampled as it heats itself; its tables then carry its static heating, and the stand-in's provenance says it was not isothermal.

A device that is not a FET

The instance line maps the terminals. For an HBT whose module is c b e, --device 'VerilogA:Q1 d g 0 File=…' puts the base on g, the collector on d and the emitter on 0. In the stand-in, vgs is then Vbe, vds is Vce, the drain current is the collector current, the gate current is the base current, and the two charges are the base and collector charges. Drain–source symmetry stays off, and the equation templates, which are FET laws, do not apply. The base–emitter junction is exponential, so sample Vbe in steps of about 5 mV where it conducts.

Tried by hand, not in a test, on a VBIC 1.3 model compiled from Verilog-A, with a generic parameter set (IS 1e-16 A, β 100, RBX 10 Ω, RBI 20 Ω, RCX 5 Ω, RCI 10 Ω, RE 2 Ω, CJE 50 fF, CJC 20 fF, TF 2 ps). Its five terminals were placed d g 0 0 t, so the substrate went to the emitter and dt to the thermal node. Vbe ran from −1 to 1.1 V in 5 mV steps and Vce from −0.5 to 7 V in 0.1 V, 31,996 points sampled isothermally in 45 s; Rth read back as the model's own 300 K/W. Load-pulled at 2 GHz, Vbe 0.82 V and Vce 3 V over 13 loads about 200 Ω, at 3 dB compression the stand-in was within 0.11 dB of the device in Pout (0.17 at worst) and 1.7 points in PAE, but 1.0 dB in Gt and 5.6° in AM–PM. The miss is on the input side, in the charges. With the base and collector resistances inside the module, the two integration paths of the Charge step disagree by 8 % for the base charge and 33 % for the collector charge, and the table takes their mean. That is the case for refitting the charges as an ANN, or for typing the resistances into the document so the core is the junctions alone. The stand-in ran the 13 loads in 1.24 s against the device's 1.73 s, and in 0.82 s on the parallel grid.

How much faster

These are hand runs, not tests: each device was load-pulled with circuitrf lp over the 20 loads of the Hero-3 grid at 2 GHz, each driven to 3 dB of Gt compression, with the stand-in placed on the same bench. The times include starting the process (0.33 s). They are the shortest of several runs on a shared machine, so read the ratios rather than the seconds. The last column has the parallel loadpull grid: it runs a stand-in's loads side by side and keeps a compiled model on its one worker.

Device Device Stand-in Parallel grid: device / stand-in
The GaN example as compiled Verilog-A (its network, traps at their DC values, ANN constituents) 4.14 s 1.81 s 3.91 / 0.92 s
The same model as its own ComposedFET 3.78 s 2.42 s
A closed-form FET, compiled 0.81 s 1.81 s 0.77 / 0.93 s
Hero-2's SDD FET, in-process 0.83 s 2.00 s 0.60 / 1.02 s

Against the compiled GaN the stand-in is 2.3× faster, and 4.3× on the parallel grid. Against a model that costs next to nothing per evaluation, a closed form whether compiled or in-process, it is slower: its tables and their extrapolation cost more than the formula. Most of the gain comes from the device's own cost per evaluation, not from the worker's round trip; the compiled GaN and its ComposedFET twin cost about the same. Make a stand-in for a device that is expensive to evaluate: a compact model with many internal nodes, or one built from networks.

Sampling the compiled GaN took 17 s for 5,265 points, one at a time, once.

The first two devices are quasi-static by construction, and the stand-in reproduces them to within the drive step. The GaN module carries its own network inside, which the stand-in collapses: its error is the phase of that network at 2 GHz and its harmonics, not the tables. model validate runs exactly this comparison against the device's own loadpull, so you see which case you are in before you use the stand-in.

Checklist

  • The device is expensive to evaluate; a closed-form model is faster as it is.
  • The bias, model parameters and frequency are the ones the circuit uses.
  • The grid spans the RF swing at compression, not only the DC bias range, and no run warns that it left the region.
  • The grid is fine where the device bends: tenths of a volt at a FET's knee, millivolts across a junction.
  • A thermal node, when there is one, is wired as t, and Cth is typed before fitting.
  • A network the device carries inside is typed into the document when its phase matters at the working frequency.
  • The Charge step's two paths agree; when they do not, the charges are refitted as an ANN.
  • model validate passes against the device's own loadpull.
